# Horizontalna mobilnost at UNIZG

The rules half of the cross-faculty question. ISVU holds the catalog (what is taught where); this file
holds what the University itself has decided about taking those courses. Read it before answering any
"can I study at a second faculty" question, in either language.

**The one thing to get right: this is an established right, not a favour anyone grants case by case.**
It is written into the *Pravilnik o studiranju Sveučilišta u Zagrebu*, article 32, and FOI's own
procedure opens by saying `studentu se odobrava ostvarenje **prava** na horizontalnu mobilnost`.
Never answer a student as though cross-faculty enrolment might not be permitted, and never tell them
to go and find out *whether* it is possible. It is. What varies is the paperwork.

## What it is, officially

> Horizontalna mobilnost podrazumijeva mogućnost studenata da tijekom svog matičnog studija upisuje
> pojedine kolegije drugih studija (studijskih programa) unutar Sveučilišta, koji se ne izvode na
> matičnom studiju, uz priznavanje položenih ispita i ostvarenih bodova prema ECTS-u.

You stay a normal student of your home faculty; nothing about that status changes. You additionally
enrol, attend and sit exams in individual courses elsewhere in UNIZG.

It also covers **foreign-language courses inside UNIZG**, including ones aimed at incoming exchange
students: a domestic student may enrol those through horizontalna mobilnost. So it overlaps with
international mobility without being Erasmus, which is a different thing (another university).

Source: *Smjernice za horizontalnu mobilnost*, Odbor za upravljanje kvalitetom, 19 November 2024,
KLASA 602-03/24-29/12.

## Conditions, and how few there are

| | |
|---|---|
| Level must match | prijediplomska / diplomska / poslijediplomska. The **level** is what governs, *not* the type of study — `mjerodavna je razina …, a ne vrsta studija (sveučilišni, stručni)`. Basis: ZVOZD art. 67(3), Zakon o HKO art. 7(6). |
| Course must not be taught on your own programme | That is the definition; a course you could take at home is not mobility. |
| Grade average | **No university-level GPA requirement at all.** Do not invent one. |
| Real constraint | **Capacity of the host sastavnica**, decided by its competent body on the course leader's proposal. |

Uptake is low — the University's own *Strategija razvoja podrške studentima* records horizontalna
mobilnost as `prisutna … ali ne u značajnijoj mjeri`. In practice that means capacity is rarely the
binding constraint, and the 2024 Smjernice exist precisely to get more students using it.

## Who approves

Three consents, and the student collects them:

1. **the competent body of your home programme** — approves the enrolment,
2. **the ECTS coordinator of the host sastavnica**,
3. **the nositelj of the chosen course**.

A dedicated `potvrda` is then issued naming the course and fixing its ECTS value. The host course
leader enters the grade and the ECTS into the information system; at FOI the course goes into ISVU
`s originalnim nazivom i originalnim brojem ECTS bodova`.

## ECTS recognition: two routes, and you have to ask

Credits earned on another programme are proved with a certified transcript. Then either:

- **counted toward the degree** — if the chosen course's learning outcomes match your home
  programme's, the credits count into the total needed for the degree and go into the
  *dopunska isprava o studiju*; or
- **recognised as additional credits** — recorded, but not counted toward the degree total.

Either way **the student starts this, through their own referada**. If you never ask, the credits
simply sit at the host faculty as a separate record. Worth saying out loud, because students assume
recognition is automatic.

## Failing costs nothing

A course taken through horizontalna mobilnost carries no penalty at the home faculty if you do not
pass it. It does not damage your home average. This is the single most useful thing to tell a student
who is unsure whether a field is for them: it makes one course a cheap experiment.

Do not tell a student the grade lands in their home programme like any other course — recognition is
opt-in, per the two routes above.

## Versus paralelni studij

| | horizontalna mobilnost | paralelni studij |
|---|---|---|
| what you enrol | individual courses you pick | the entire second programme |
| degrees | one | two |
| entry bar | no GPA requirement | strict; FER's pravilnik sets 4.5 |
| failing a course | no consequence at home | normal consequences |

You can move **from** horizontalna mobilnost **to** paralelni studij later, so starting with a course
or two closes nothing off.

## Why students cannot find this written down

The Smjernice audited every sastavnica's website in 2024 and found, in the University's own words:
most publish no detailed enrolment conditions online; only a minority document the approval
procedure; ECTS-recognition information is rare and buried inside pravilnici; capacity limits are
`gotovo uopće nije dostupna online`; information is often stale, and some faculties link only
*international* mobility. Hence the recommendations to centralise, standardise and label the sections.

**So: a faculty having no page is evidence about that faculty's website, not about the student's
rights.** Say that, rather than letting the gap read as a prohibition.

Named good practice: **Farmaceutsko-biokemijski fakultet**, **Pravni fakultet**,
**Fakultet organizacije i informatike**.

## The process, concretely

University level, from the Smjernice, plus a worked FER→PMF case (one student, 2025/26 — illustrative,
not normative):

1. **Choose courses.** Browse the programmes that interest you and read the actual syllabi; a lot of
   what looks interesting turns out not to be. Order candidates by prerequisites and decide which
   year each would go in. This is the part ISVU answers — see the tools.
2. **Check it physically fits.** Lay the host timetable over your home one. If it does not fit, drop
   a course or push it a year. Exam collisions happen too; expect one eventually.
3. **Contact the nositelj** of the course and agree how you will attend and sit the exam. FOI's
   procedure has the student do this explicitly, in cooperation with the home ECTS coordinator.
4. **Ask your own referada** what they need. At FER, nothing was required of the student; elsewhere
   there may be a home-side form.
5. **Apply to the host faculty's referada**: what you want to enrol and why (a short motivation),
   plus a `potvrda o studiranju` and the faculty's official `molba` form from its website. PMF
   charged €7 for the molba. Fees and forms are per-faculty.
6. **Time it to the regular enrolment window.** FOI requires it `prije upisa u semestar u kojem
   ostvaruje mobilnost`. Mailing the referada during normal enrolment is when they can actually
   enrol you; mailing them outside it mostly just annoys them. Some faculties close roughly a week
   after teaching starts — *unverified, varies, tell the student to ask*.
7. You then show up on **Studomat with two faculties**.

Hidden cost worth mentioning when the two faculties are far apart: transport. FOI states plainly
that the student bears travel costs if the course is not taught in Varaždin.

## What to tell the student to confirm, and what not to

Confirm with the referada: **the form, the fee, the deadline, and whether the course has room.**

Do not send them off to confirm whether cross-faculty study is allowed, whether they need a certain
average, or whether their faculty "permits" it. Those are settled at university level.
